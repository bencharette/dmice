{\bfseries Can the Muon Background Fake a Dark Matter Signal?}\\
{\bfseries Extending DM-Ice17's Annual-Modulation Systematics into the WIMP Signal Region}\\[2pt]
{\normalsize Ben Charette \quad$\cdot$\quad Faculty Mentor: Prof.\ Carlos A.\ Arg\"uelles Delgado, Department of Physics}
\end{center}

\vspace{-6pt}

\section{Objective, Significance, and Implications}

For more than two decades the DAMA/LIBRA experiment in Italy has reported that its
low-energy event rate rises and falls once a year, peaking near June~2, and has
interpreted this as the Earth's orbit sweeping through a galactic halo of dark matter
particles. No other experiment has reproduced it, and the central objection is
mundane: laboratories also have seasons. Temperature, radon, and cosmic-ray muon flux
all vary annually, and any of them could imprint a yearly cycle on a sodium-iodide
detector. Distinguishing a galactic signal from an environmental one therefore comes
down to \emph{phase}---a dark matter signal keeps its June maximum everywhere on
Earth, while most terrestrial effects follow the local seasons and invert in the
southern hemisphere.

DM-Ice17 was built to make exactly that comparison. It is a pair of 8.5\,kg
thallium-doped NaI crystals frozen into the Antarctic ice at 2450\,m depth, at the
bottom of two IceCube Neutrino Observatory strings, and it is the only NaI dark
matter detector ever operated inside a cubic-kilometre muon telescope---which means
its dominant seasonal background can be tagged independently, event by event, by the
surrounding array.

Over the past summer I established that the DM-Ice muon \emph{flux} modulates by
$10.2\% \pm 1.6\%$ and peaks in late January (day-of-year $30 \pm 9$, $6.2\sigma$),
$13.4\sigma$ away from the DAMA phase, and I recovered a muon selection for the
2015--2020 high-gain era that no published analysis had ever reached. The flux,
however, is not what a dark matter search actually sees. What enters the 2--6\,keV
electron-equivalent (keVee) signal region is the \emph{delayed} tail of muon energy
deposition---scintillator afterglow and phosphorescence with its own time
structure---plus the ordinary low-energy radioactive background. My objective this
term is to measure that directly: \textbf{does the low-energy background inside the
WIMP region of interest modulate, at what amplitude and phase, how much of it is
muon-induced, and how much of that the IceCube veto removes?}

This matters for three reasons. First, it converts a statement about muons into a
statement about a dark matter search: an ROI modulation amplitude and phase is the
quantity a DAMA test is actually made of, and it has never been measured for a
southern-hemisphere NaI detector. Second, the muon-induced leakage fraction and the
corresponding veto rejection efficiency are the first such numbers for a NaI target
inside an active muon detector, and they are a concrete design input for future
in-ice or veto-instrumented NaI deployments in the DM-Ice$\rightarrow$COSINE-100
program. Third, the underlying dataset gains lasting value: every published DM-Ice
muon analysis stops in January 2015, two months after the photomultiplier gain was
raised roughly tenfold, so the majority of the exposure has never been
characterized. Completing that characterization is a publishable result in its own
right and the prerequisite for any use of the 2015--2020 data.

\section{Preparation Completed}

This is a continuation, not a new start, and the load-bearing infrastructure already
exists and is validated. I have built a stitched waveform-summary extraction over the
full 2011--2022 DM-Ice run (2.38 billion events reduced to 136 columnar files), which
solves the problem that high-gain signals span three digitizer channels with no single
channel covering the full gamma-to-muon range. I built the first real DM-Ice live-time
database in the group's code base---per-run active spans binned by day of year across
95{,}174 runs, yielding 1635 low-gain and 2328 high-gain live-days per
detector---replacing the calendar-day approximation every prior script used; live-time
normalization is the load-bearing systematic for any modulation fit. I reproduced the
published pulse-shape muon populations as a baseline
(2.89--2.92 muons/crystal/day), then diagnosed and fixed a $751\times$ over-count when
that selection was transferred to the high-gain era, reaching
$2.918 \pm 0.038$\,muons/crystal/day at efficiency 0.949 and purity 0.933---statistically
indistinguishable from the low-gain benchmark at $0.03\sigma$---using a discriminant
never previously applied to these data. I also hold a frozen 218-event
IceCube-coincidence muon sample and a low-gain ADC-to-keV calibration validated by the
600\,keV thallium/bismuth line. What remains is a well-scoped extension of a working
pipeline.

\section{Detailed Research Plan}

\noindent\textbf{Work Package 1 --- High-gain low-energy calibration (the current
blocker).} In the high-gain era, low-energy events are digitized in the high-gain
ATWD0 channel, but my existing summary columns carry the low-gain ATWD2 channel, which
is empty for $\sim$97\% of those events; the 2--6\,keVee region is therefore simply
absent from the current reduction. I will complete the ATWD0-equivalent extraction
(already written and tested on a 20-file-per-month subsample for 2013 and 2017), run it
over 2015--2020 on IceCube's Cobalt/Condor infrastructure, and fit per-era,
per-detector energy scales anchored on the 600\,keV Tl/Bi and 1460\,keV $^{40}$K gamma
lines. \emph{Deliverable:} a trustworthy keV scale and a defensible ROI definition in
both gain eras. \emph{Validation:} the same procedure applied to 2013 must reproduce
the independently verified low-gain calibration.

\noindent\textbf{Work Package 2 --- ROI modulation fit.} On live-time-normalized
day-of-year histograms of non-muon-tagged events with 2--6\,keVee deposits, I will fit
$R(t) = R_0[1 + A\cos(2\pi(t - t_0)/365)]$ separately per detector and per gain era,
since selection thresholds differ. Fixing all cuts before examining the phase protects
against tuning toward an answer, and the two eras---different gain, different readout
channel, different years---serve as each other's cross-check. \emph{Deliverable:} $A$
and $t_0$ with uncertainties, compared against the DAMA phase (day 152) and the known
austral-summer in-ice muon maximum (day 15--30); a null result is reported as an
amplitude limit.

\noindent\textbf{Work Package 3 --- Afterglow leakage and veto efficiency.} I will
measure the rate of ROI events in delayed windows following each tagged muon, from
microseconds to seconds, against randomized control windows drawn from the same runs,
which isolates the muon-correlated excess and its decay time. Combining that leakage
fraction with the IceCube-coincident sample gives the veto rejection efficiency for
muon-induced ROI events and the dead-time cost of applying it---the number that closes
the loop from ``muons modulate'' to ``muons contribute $X\%$ of the ROI modulation, of
which the veto removes $Y\%$.''

\noindent\textbf{Work Package 4 --- Two open systematics.} The 2015 high-gain Det-1
muon rate sits $12.5\sigma$ below 2016--2020 and is demonstrably not a selection
artifact; I will test the leading hypothesis---that per-run live-time is overestimated
because it cannot resolve intra-run dead time---by comparing per-run event-rate and
span distributions against later years. Separately, an unidentified high-amplitude
population in high-gain Det-1 (bimodal decay parameter at 173 and 182\,ns, and
confirmed not to be digitizer saturation) is a $^{210}$Po alpha candidate that becomes
testable once WP1's calibration exists.

\noindent\textbf{Risks and fallbacks.} If the full re-extraction proves too slow on
shared storage, I will restrict WP2 to Det-2 and 2016--2020, a subsample already shown
to be clean and year-stable, which preserves the physics result at reduced statistics.
If the 2--6\,keVee band turns out to be dominated by the trigger turn-on, I will raise
the band to 6--20\,keVee and report the phase there plus a limit below. All selections
live in a single canonical module so that no two scripts can disagree about a cut, and
IceCube coincidence remains available throughout as an independent hard label.

I plan on 10--12 hours per week; long extractions run unattended on Cobalt/NPX, so
compute time does not compete with coursework.

\noindent\textbf{Weeks 1--3 (Sep 8--26):} finalize the ATWD0-equivalent extraction and
validate it on 2013 low-gain data, where the correct answer is already known.

\noindent\textbf{Weeks 4--6 (Sep 29--Oct 17):} full 2015--2020 extraction on Condor;

\section{Faculty Involvement and Support Network}

My faculty mentor is Prof.\ Carlos A.\ Arg\"uelles Delgado (Department of Physics),
who leads Harvard's IceCube group and has supervised this project throughout. He sets
the physics scope and priorities, reviews results at each milestone, and has previously
required me to freeze and defend a completed analysis in writing before moving
on---a practice that produced the wrap-up memo underpinning this proposal. He
supervises through weekly group meetings and regular one-on-one meetings, provides the
IceCube Collaboration channel through which this work receives internal review, and
provides the computing access the project depends on: IceCube's Cobalt and NPX systems
and the group's GPU allocation on the FAS research cluster.

Day-to-day technical supervision comes from the group's postdoctoral researcher, Felix
Yu, who has defined several of the concrete tasks I have completed---including the
simulation energy-floor characterization and the IceCube MuonFilter overlap check---and
who advises on reconstruction and machine-learning methods. Beyond the group, the
IceCube Collaboration's internal review process provides external scrutiny before any
result is published, and the DM-Ice and COSINE-100 literature, especially A.~J.~F.\
Hubbard's 2015 thesis, provides the published pulse-shape baseline against which every
step of my selection is validated. I also maintain written session handoffs across the
three machines this analysis runs on, which makes my work auditable by my mentor and
reproducible by whoever inherits it.
